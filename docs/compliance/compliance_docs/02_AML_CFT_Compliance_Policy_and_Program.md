## 1. Purpose

This policy documents the board-approved, risk-based Anti-Money Laundering / Combating the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) program of **Xego** for the WhatsApp/Telme payment platform. It establishes the minimum standards for customer identification, due diligence, record keeping, reporting and training to prevent the use of the platform for money laundering, terrorism financing and other financial crimes.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all customers, transactions, products and services offered by Xego, and to all directors, staff, agents and third parties acting for or on behalf of Xego.

## 3. Program Components

### 3.1 Governance and Appointment

- A **Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)** at General Manager level or above is formally appointed by the Board to implement and oversee the program.
- An **AML/CFT Oversight Committee** (see the AML/CFT Oversight Committee Charter) meets at planned intervals and escalates material matters to the Board.
- The Board approves this policy, the risk assessment and any material changes.

### 3.2 Institutional Risk Assessment

- Xego maintains a documented ML/FT risk assessment covering country, customer, product, service and channel risks.
- The assessment is refreshed at least every 12–18 months and on material change.

### 3.3 Customer Identification Program (CIP)

- No account or transaction service is opened without verified customer identity.
- Identity verification uses government-issued documents and a licensed Nigerian identity verification provider (e.g., Smile ID, Youverify) integrated into the platform.
- The KYC ladder (L0–L3) is enforced; higher tiers unlock higher limits. Tier-1 details are recorded as a customer declaration, and identity/screening outcomes come from real providers rather than a simulated approval.

### 3.4 Customer Due Diligence (CDD) and Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

- **Standard CDD**: verify identity, address, occupation and purpose of the account/transactions before onboarding.
- **Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)**: apply additional checks for higher-risk customers, complex or unusually large transactions, and customers from high-risk jurisdictions.
- **Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)**: identify PEPs at onboarding and through ongoing screening; require senior management approval to establish or continue relationships; subject to enhanced ongoing monitoring.
- **Beneficial ownership**: identify and verify beneficial owners of legal-person customers.

### 3.5 Sanctions and Watchlist Screening

- All customers and beneficial owners are screened against OFAC, UN, EU and CBN-referenced sanctions lists at onboarding and periodically thereafter.
- Matches are handled per procedure: verification, freeze/refusal of service, and reporting.

### 3.6 Monitoring and Reporting

- A transaction monitoring rules engine alerts on indicators of money laundering (structuring, rapid pass-through, mismatched source of funds, etc.).
- **Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs)** are filed to NFIU and the CBN within 24 hours of detection; an internal Suspicious Transaction Register is maintained.
- **Currency Transaction Reports (CTRs)** are filed at/above the prescribed threshold; a Currency Transaction Register is maintained.
- **PEP transaction returns** are submitted to the CBN monthly.
- No tipping-off: staff must not disclose to a customer that they are subject to an STR/CTR or investigation.

### 3.7 Record Keeping

- All customer identification, transaction and communication records are retained for the statutory period (minimum 7 years or as prescribed by the CBN) and made available to regulators on request.

### 3.8 Training

- Appropriate personnel receive AML/CFT training on hire and at least annually, covering indicators, obligations, reporting and record keeping.
- Training is documented (attendees, dates, materials, test results) by HR/Compliance.

### 3.9 Independent Testing

- The program is independently tested (internal audit or external auditors) at least every 12–18 months; findings are remediated and reported to the Board.

## 4. Compliance and Enforcement

- Compliance with this policy is mandatory. Breaches are investigated and may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination and regulatory reporting.
- The CCO has authority to require remedial action and to escalate to the Board.

## 5. Review

This policy is reviewed at least every 12–18 months and upon material change to business, technology or regulation.
